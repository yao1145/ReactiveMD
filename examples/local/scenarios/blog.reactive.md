# Rereading The Back View, I noticed the coat first

Lin's reading notebook · Literary reflection and a personal note · Sample blog

While sorting old books, I found my school copy of Zhu Ziqing's The Back View. My underlining was still there. I could almost recite the passage about the oranges, but this time I stopped when the father brushed the dirt from his clothes.

At school, I knew where I was supposed to feel moved. Reading it now, I wanted to set those familiar conclusions aside for a moment. I watched someone walk, turn around and say something important in an ordinary voice.

## Don't define the father too quickly

At the station, the father minds the luggage, bargains and asks an attendant to look after his son. The younger narrator finds this unnecessary and dislikes the way his father speaks. The essay keeps that impatience. The farewell doesn't begin as an uncomplicated scene of affection.

That gives the later tears somewhere to come from. The person telling the story can now see what his younger self missed, but he leaves that young man, eager to prove his independence, in the scene. I feel a little embarrassed for him as I read.

## I'm glad the awkwardness remains

This time I also noticed the account of the father's temper and the distance between father and son. The moment at the station doesn't settle everything that has been difficult between them.

I wanted to reread it because of that. Care and stubbornness can belong to the same person. Recognising a parent's effort doesn't immediately teach a child how to speak well to them. The narrator looks back after years have passed; understanding arrives slowly, and some chances to say something have gone.

That unfinished feeling stays with me longer than a lesson already neatly stated.

## Closing the book, I remembered a lunch bag

Once, before travelling back from home, I took out the insulated lunch bag my mother had added to an already full backpack. She didn't insist. I could buy something on the way if I got hungry, she said.

At the station I discovered a small packet of biscuits in a side pocket. Nothing dramatic happened. I ate them. Rereading The Back View brought that little incident back to me. Some responses seem to arrive long after the event.

I don't want every call home to become solemn. After dinner the other day, I sent a photo of something I'd cooked. My mother asked whether I'd used too much salt. I laughed, and we went on to talk about the vegetable shop near my flat.

## I'll stop here for now

I didn't underline the old copy again. I flattened a folded page corner. Next time, perhaps I'll notice the family circumstances at the beginning, or the letter near the end. For now, I remember the father putting down the oranges and brushing his clothes.
